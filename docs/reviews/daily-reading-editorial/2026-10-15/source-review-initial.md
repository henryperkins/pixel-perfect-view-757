## Proposed guidance versus current local policy

All six responses are low-stakes, limited, and connected to their reading: draft one sentence, offer a dinner preference, bound one project commitment, name an interest, distinguish words from interpretation, or enjoy a song. None asks for a consequential life decision, predicts success, or insists that the transit makes action necessary. Under the **proposed** standard, those optional invitations are suitable once the source and wording issues above are resolved. Their optional form does not resolve the separate current policy conflict.

The inspected local production-writing contract says “Never … instruct” (`daily-voice.ts:54`). Its warm tone does not authorize assumed feelings or reassurance (`daily-voice.ts:75`), and its source rules require preserved conditions, modality, and causal direction (`daily-voice.ts:50–55,105–111`). The default bundle describes copy as observational and never instructional (`default-bundle.ts:1–7`). The current review protocol disallows instructions (`reviewer-instructions.md:25–28`), and its quiet check explicitly requires no instruction (`reviewer-instructions.md:59–62`).

Therefore all six contain unresolved current-policy conflicts through their optional exercises or suggestions. Case 1 additionally has a command-form headline. Relational reassurance, self-description permission, and permission to leave uncertainty open also need deliberate alignment with the current warm-voice constraints. This review has not modified or waived any of those rules. Current-policy failure and an editorial-exploration revision are different judgments.

The exact source extensions, human headlines, revised reflections, and quiet copy are not currently supplied runtime facts. Even if the guidance policy changed, these drafts would not automatically be source-compatible. Current paragraph/source citation requirements and synthesis parent coverage would still need to be implemented and verified.
