# Quiet-day variation — proposed editorial repertoire

The old quiet rule narrowed an ordinary invitation to enjoyment and required announcing the missing signal. Human review rejected that approach. The proposed rule is now: **choose an ordinary observation with its own distinction, leave the day's emotional character undescribed, and attach no astrological explanation.** No runtime behaviour changes here.

The current sixth reading is one example, not a reusable body for every quiet day. These are outlines for later source authoring, not extra approved readings:

| Editorial possibility | Observable entry | Distinction | Possible direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Detail without a message — current example | A bright thread in a dark coat. | Noticing versus interpreting. | Let a second look remain a look. |
| A pause before another activity | A kettle waiting to boil while a hand reaches for a phone. | An available moment versus an obligation to fill it. | Wait without automatically replacing the pause. |
| A question with no ready answer | Someone stays with a question instead of immediately offering an example. | Curiosity versus arriving with a conclusion. | Ask or listen before supplying an answer. |
| An experience without a record | A song ends before its title has been found. | Having an experience versus keeping a record of it. | Enjoy or remember without turning it into a capture task. |

Any later catalogue must preserve these distinctions and review each full draft. Choosing an editorial variant by rotation or history must not be represented as a fresh chart-derived message. Saved data or personalization would need a separate implementation decision. This pack demonstrates one revised quiet voice; it does not claim demonstrated day-to-day variation.
