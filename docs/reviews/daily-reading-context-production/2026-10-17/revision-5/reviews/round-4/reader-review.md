## Dimensions

- **Recognition 4.** A reader can place the scene: notes for a familiar task turning into an outline, and an afternoon whose hours are "already spoken for". But "the next piece" never becomes something the reader can picture.
- **Meaning 4.** Both contacts are translated into the scene, and the body names a useful distinction between how easily a task grows and the capacity to carry it. The sentence that names it is abstract, though, and "structure" is never given a practical form.
- **Direction 4.** The closing step is optional, small and concrete, and it returns to the opening image. The sentence before it competes with it by permitting several pieces to keep going.
- **Distinctness 3 (blocking).** The second paragraph opens by restating the first paragraph's tension in vaguer words, then stacks two new conditional frames. The central idea is repeated and crowded instead of developed.
- **Voice 4.** Calm, with no counseling, dictated script or appended personality, and some exact plain phrasing. Held back by dense hedging (eight "can" in six authored sentences, plus "may" and "could"), a formal qualifier and an aphoristic line about curiosity.

Chronology: the fixed sentence is given practical meaning, but late. It follows two named contacts with no bridge, and only the final sentence connects it ("an earlier way of handling the task", "this return", "one smaller change"). This limits the body but does not block it.

## Blocking evidence

1. **Distinctness:** "A bigger task can need more structure to carry it, however easy starting feels, and that need can show up where the task grows past what it was set up for."
   This makes the same point in different words. "however easy starting feels" re-says "adding the next piece can feel natural". "more structure to carry it" re-says "the task itself can take more effort than usual". "where the task grows past what it was set up for" re-says the notes "turning into an outline". The paragraph that should move the tension forward opens by repeating it.
2. **Distinctness:** "And if an earlier way of handling the task no longer fits"
   This is crowding. After the restatement, the paragraph goes on as stacked conditionals ("If more than one piece is competing for the afternoon", then "And if ..."), each opening a new frame. Seven sentences carry two contacts, the chronology, a distinction, a curiosity point and a method change, and no idea gets a second sentence. The wording repeats too: "the next piece" appears three times and "more than one" twice in one sentence.

## Limiting evidence

- **Recognition:** "The notes for a task you know well can start turning into an outline." The image is recognizable, but it is posed as a possibility about an unnamed familiar task instead of a noticed detail.
- **Recognition:** "the next piece needs hours that are already spoken for". "The next piece" carries the central tension but is never made concrete (a section, an idea, a second project?). Later it shifts to competing options ("more than one piece is competing for the afternoon").
- **Meaning:** "and that need can show up where the task grows past what it was set up for". This places the need only in the abstract. "Structure" gets no practical form, and the closing step answers it by limiting scope, a link the reader has to make.
- **Meaning:** "This is the second of three calculated contacts; the first exact contact falls in September 2026." It comes after two named contacts, so the reader has to work out which one "This" refers to (the placement suggests the Saturn contact). Its practical meaning arrives three sentences later, and only as a condition.
- **Direction:** "If more than one piece is competing for the afternoon, you may not need to decide between them yet: following more than one line of curiosity can help a direction take shape." The first paragraph says the afternoon's hours are "already spoken for", yet this permits several pieces to keep going, which pulls against the closing step. The reader has to reconcile the two (explore in notes, commit in the outline). The closing step itself is the body's strongest line: "the next piece can wait as a note instead of joining the outline".
- **Direction:** "you could use this return to try one smaller change". It never says what the change is smaller than.
- **Voice:** "when the aim is well chosen". A formal qualifier that is never picked up. The reader is not told what aim, or how to tell whether it is well chosen.
- **Voice:** "following more than one line of curiosity can help a direction take shape". Aphoristic and abstract, and it sits beside mild reassurance in "you may not need to decide between them yet".
- **Voice:** "A bigger task can need more structure to carry it". This shows the hedging. Every authored sentence uses a modal, so the body supposes instead of noticing. The distinction sentence, where the voice should be most exact, is the most abstract.
