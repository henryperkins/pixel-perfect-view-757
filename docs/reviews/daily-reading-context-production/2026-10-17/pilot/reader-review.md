## R1 — Fail

The afternoon, additional check and extra version give this a recognizable situation. The distinction between moving a commitment and changing its scope is useful, and leaving an addition out is appropriately modest direction.

Best passage: “Moving that work into a later calendar opening may leave the extra check attached”. It makes the problem visible without inventing a feeling or personal history.

Blockers: the second paragraph restates that distinction as “Putting the commitment into a different slot does not change what it includes.” The recurrence detail leads to a small adjustment, so it has some practical meaning, but it does not give the body enough further movement to justify the repeated explanation. “Where expectations exceed the capacity available, its boundary may need to become clearer” sounds like an administrative account of a problem. “Make a stopping point visible before an extra version enters” continues that procedural register. The body is competent advice with a concrete example; the requested observer voice is not sustained.

## R2 — Fail

There are useful ideas here: a second idea can stay a note; beginning more work and carrying it are different capacities. The directions are optional and proportionate. The afternoon and extra check provide a sufficient point of recognition.

Best passage: “a note can hold a second idea without promising it those hours.”

Blockers: this reads as several short readings assembled in sequence. It moves from rescheduling to boundaries, from competing projects to calculated chronology, then into initiative and expansion. The chronology closes the first paragraph without changing the reader's understanding of what preceded it. The second paragraph repeats ease of starting versus capacity to continue in adjacent sentences. “When an aim is well chosen, practical initiative can make another piece of the same project start to look appealing to take up” is formal and laborious. The concepts are understandable individually, but the body does not make them one clear observation. That is a meaning and distinctness problem, not a penalty for containing more context.

## R3 — Pass, at the threshold

The body supplies both a visible addition to a task and a visible division of attention: a page of notes beside an open tab. It develops two related decisions within the same afternoon: what fits inside the responsibility, and whether another interest must become a commitment. The second decision adds something to the first instead of explaining the scheduling point again.

Best passage: “a note can hold the second idea without promising it the rest of the afternoon.” This is concrete, optional and proportionate. “Exploring possible directions and choosing one project to pursue can be separate stages” supplies a usable distinction.

No below-threshold blocker. The sentence about expectations, capacity and a clearer boundary is still too formal, and “If several projects happen to compete” is a slightly mechanical transition. The concrete notes/tab scene and precise final option carry enough of the body to sustain a 4 for voice. This is an acceptable body, not an exceptional one, and the pass should not be rounded up to a stronger claim.
