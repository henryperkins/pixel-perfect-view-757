## What changed and what the pilot found

The [five material cards](../../../../../src/lib/ai/context-writing-material.json) now separate source-bound scene details, practical distinctions and optional directions from conditions and local source/review proof. An independent source review passed the exact material bytes before deployment. The provider receives the projected building blocks, not full legacy source paragraphs, review annotations, benchmark bodies, headlines or reflections. Original source facts remain available to validation. The same five cases, source bundle, benchmarks, provider and transport limits were retained.

The generated bodies now give the reader something more specific to consider: an extra check changing a task's scope, a second idea held in notes, or a stopping point instead of another calendar slot. The reader rated direction 4/5 in every case. The source reviewer rated application of additional context 4/5 in all four applicable cases, compared with 2/5 in the first pilot. These are judgments from different independent AI reviewers, not a paired A/B study, human approval or member-outcome evidence. The reader threshold is at least 4 in every dimension. All five miss voice. Formal phrases such as “established responsibility,” “available capacity” and “inside the commitment” still lead or explain the scene. Several sentences explain a distinction after an object or action has already made it visible. The combined and synthesis bodies also move between meanings instead of letting one observed moment carry them. The source reviewer credits practical application within a shared workload; the reader's separate judgment concerns how coherently and naturally that application reads.

The synthesis close changes **“can increase”** into **“quickly increases.”** It makes a possible relationship definite and adds unsupported speed. Complete source parents and automatic acceptance did not catch that change. The contact-only body's “may quickly exceed” and natal body's “can easily slip” also add unsupported emphasis, although their hypothetical scope survives. Four bodies have no major grounding defect; that does not mean all four are free of wording concerns.

All five preserve supplied parent references and required chronology. None repeats the first pilot's unsupported recent-onset claim. The added guard addresses that exact known phrase; it does not establish general semantic correctness. The bodies also omit named planetary contacts and imagery found in the authored benchmarks. This pilot therefore does not qualify the benchmark's astrological imagery or establish that the writing now matches the requested voice.
